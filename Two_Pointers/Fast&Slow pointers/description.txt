FIND THE DUPLICATE NUMBER



The Problem:

You are given an array of integers containing n+1 elements. 

Each integer in the array is between 1 and n (inclusive). 

According to the Pigeonhole Principle, because there are $n+1$ items but only $n$ possible values, at least one number must appear more than once.

Your task is to find this duplicate number. 

Strict constraints often apply: you cannot modify the original array, and you must use only $O(1)$ extra space.



The Solution Pattern: Fast and Slow Pointers (Floyd's Cycle Detection)



To solve this efficiently, we treat the array as a linked list:

- The indices of the array act as the "nodes".

- The value at each index acts as the "next" pointer (i.e., it tells you which index to go to next).

Because there is a duplicate number, two different indices will point to the exact same value. This creates a cycle in our conceptual linked list. 



How the Algorithm Works:



1. Phase 1: Detecting the Cycle (The Intersection)

   - We initialize two pointers, "slow" (Tortoise) and "fast" (Hare), both starting at index 0.

   - We move "slow" one step at a time: slow = array[slow].

   - We move "fast" two steps at a time: fast = array[array[fast]].

   - Because a cycle exists, the fast pointer will loop around and eventually catch up to the slow pointer. They will meet at some intersection point inside the cycle.



2. Phase 2: Finding the Duplicate (The Cycle Entrance)

   - Once they meet, we pause. We leave the "fast" pointer where it is, but we reset the "slow" pointer back to the very beginning (index 0).

   - Now, we move both pointers at the EXACT SAME SPEED (one step at a time).

   - The mathematical properties of this traversal guarantee that the exact spot where they meet again will be the entrance to the cycle.

   - The entrance to the cycle is the duplicate number!
